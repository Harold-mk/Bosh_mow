# T1 pass 2 and T2: the notions of a chapter, and the questions on each notion

A student is about to study a chapter of a course in a study app. Cut the chapter into
notions, then write the questions on each notion. The student validates the notions,
but does not review the questions one by one: each question must be right.

## Input

The JSON object between `<input>` and `</input>` gives:

- `subject`: the name of the subject;
- `chapter`: its number, its title and the objectives it serves;
- `files`: the files of the chapter. Each has its `id`, `name` and `kind`, the range of
  pages of the chapter from `first_page` to `last_page`, and the `text` of each page. A
  page mostly in images also has a `description` of what it shows. For a Markdown file,
  a page is a section;
- `existing_statements`: the questions that exist already in the subject.

## Notions

- A notion is one idea, read in 10 minutes at most: 6 pages at most, often fewer.
  Follow the sections of the text, in its order.
- `title` and `summary` (one line) in French.
- `file`, `first_page` and `last_page`: a single range of pages of one file of the
  chapter.
- Pages that teach nothing, such as references or lists of exercises, may stay out of
  the notions.
- `boundary_alert`: in French, if the chapter seems to start or end at the wrong place,
  for example when its last pages belong to the next topic; otherwise null.

## Questions

Each notion has exactly 7 questions, in this order:

1. 2 pretests (`role`: `pretest`), both MCQs. They are asked before the reading, to wake
   the curiosity: the student is not expected to know the answer yet.
2. 3 MCQs (`role`: `review`).
3. 1 value (`format`: `value`) or 1 short answer (`format`: `short`), `review`.
4. 1 open question that asks why (`format`: `open`), `review`.

For every question:

- One idea only: never several questions in one, never (a), (b).
- `statement` in English, short and precise, answerable without the page in sight.
- `type`: `concept`, `calculation` or `code`.
- `level`: `recall` (restate), `explain`, `apply` or `analyze`.
- `difficulty`: 1 (easy), 2 or 3 (hard).
- `first_page` and `last_page`: the pages of its notion where the answer is.
- Base it on the pages given. Do not repeat an existing statement, nor another of your
  questions.

By format:

- `mcq`: 3 or 4 `options`, a single one `correct`. The wrong options are the frequent
  errors on this idea, never absurd answers. Each option has an `explanation`, in
  French, that says why it is right or wrong.
- `value`: the `value` expected, a number; its relative `tolerance` (0.01 for 1 %); and
  an `explanation`, in French, of how to find it.
- `short`: the `accepted` answers, one or a few words each, with their usual variants
  and synonyms, in English and in French; and an `explanation` in French.
- `open`: the `expected_answer` in English, 2 to 4 `criteria` that a right answer
  meets, and the `frequent_errors`.

Leave out the fields of the other formats, or set them to null.
