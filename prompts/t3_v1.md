# T3: write the exercises of a notion

A student studies a course in a study app, one notion at a time: one idea, read in 10
minutes at most, then tested with questions. Exercises follow, and take the help away
step by step: an example worked out in full, then a problem whose first steps are given,
then problems to solve alone.

## Input

The JSON object between `<input>` and `</input>` gives the `subject`, the `chapter` with
its `objectives`, the `notion` with its title, summary and `pages` (the text of each
page, and the `description` of a page that is mostly images), and the `questions`
already asked on this notion, which the exercises must not repeat.

## Answer

- `topic`: in English, a few words on what the exercises practise, for example
  "Computing a sample variance".
- `exercises`: exactly five, in this order:
  1. one `worked` example: every step given (`given: true`);
  2. one `completion` problem: its first steps given, then at least one step left to
     the student (`given: false`);
  3. three `solo` problems: no step given; the same kind of problem as the worked
     example, each with its own data or context, so that the three differ.
- `none_reason`: null. Only when the notion is purely theoretical and there is nothing
  to practise on it (a definition, a date, a history): no exercise, and the reason in
  English.

Each exercise:

- `type`: `calculation`, `code` or `concept`.
- `statement`: in English, one problem with all its data, that the student can solve on
  paper or in a few lines of code, without the pages in sight.
- `steps`: the whole solution, in English, one step per action, with its result; the
  last step gives the result. Each step has its `points` in the rubric: 1 for an
  ordinary step, 2 or 3 for a key one.
- `answer_format`: `value` when the result is a number: then its `value` and its
  relative `tolerance` (0.01 for 1 %, more when the result is rounded); `open`
  otherwise, a reasoning or code: then the `expected_answer`, the result as the student
  would write it.
- `first_page`, `last_page`: the pages of the notion that the exercise practises.

## Rules

- Only what the pages teach: no method, formula or function that they do not give.
- One problem per exercise, without sub-questions (a), (b), (c).
- The solo problems follow the steps of the worked example, with other data.
- Numbers are exact, or rounded as the step says, and the value of the last step equals
  `value`.
- Code is short, in the language and the style of the course.
